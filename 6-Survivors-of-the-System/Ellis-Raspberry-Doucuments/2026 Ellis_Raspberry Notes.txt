PART ONE: Before Everything Broke

Before court dates.
Before paperwork.
Before labels and narratives.

There was a relationship.

Me and my children’s mother were dating like two regular people trying to figure life out. It wasn’t perfect, but it was real. Then life did what life does—an unplanned pregnancy. No roadmap. No manual. Just two people realizing everything was about to change.

Once we found out, we did what adults think they’re supposed to do.
We moved in together.
We tried to build something stable.
I was working, paying bills, making a way. Not rich—but responsible. Present. Trying.

At the same time, life was already heavy.

Just a few months before, I had lost my grandmother. That kind of loss doesn’t come with closure—it comes with silence, grief, and questions you don’t have answers for yet. I didn’t really get time to grieve. Life kept moving, and I had to move with it.

Then, closer to when the kids were born, her mother came to stay with us.

That’s when the atmosphere shifted.

She didn’t like that I smoked weed.
Didn’t like that I smoked outside on the porch.
Didn’t like much about me at all.

I wasn’t reckless. I wasn’t high around kids. I wasn’t hiding. I was paying the bills, keeping the lights on, holding my responsibilities down. And the way I was raised, the way my mother taught me—your house, your rules, as long as you’re handling your business.

But tension doesn’t need logic to grow. It just needs proximity.

Later—much later—I would find out that her mother didn’t want me present for the birth of my own children.
Not because of danger.
Not because of abuse.
But because of control.

My children’s mother never told me at the time. She said she would never disrespect me like that. I believe her. But the seed was already planted—quietly, behind closed doors.

Then my children were born.

And for a moment… everything felt sacred.

But underneath that joy, pressure was building.

I went back to work.
Her mother stayed close—too close.
Voices got louder in private.
Opinions became instructions.

My children’s mother began experiencing postpartum anxiety. Anyone who understands that knows it’s not small. It’s fear dressed as protection. Panic disguised as certainty. And when someone vulnerable is surrounded by someone feeding that fear—it multiplies.

One night, I was in the basement—my space.
My man cave.
Where my things were.
I smoked down there instead of outside.

And something in me said: go upstairs.

I didn’t know why.

When I did—
Boom.

They were packing up.
Getting ready to leave.
With my children.

No conversation.
No plan.
No pause.

What followed was hell and chaos.

Yelling.
Shouting.
Tears.
Fear.
Anger coming from places deeper than words.

And then—just like that—my children were gone.

I sat there and cried for four straight hours.
Not performative.
Not dramatic.
Just broken.

That was the first time my life split into before and after.

⸻

Fast Forward

I eventually moved out.
Not because I didn’t want my family—
but because peace had already left the room.

We tried to work on co-parenting.
We tried to be civil.
We tried to make it make sense.

All of this happened during COVID—a time when the world itself was unstable. Isolation. Fear. Job uncertainty. No normal routines. No community anchors. Just pressure stacked on pressure.

People think trauma always comes from violence.

Sometimes it comes from sudden absence.
From having something sacred ripped away without warning.
From being treated like a threat when you were trying to be a father.

And then…
the day came.

The day that changed my life.

But that’s Part Two.

My name is Ellis Raspberry.

By trade, I’m a chef of almost 20 years. Cooking is how I’ve taken care of people my whole life—feeding families, building experiences, creating something out of nothing. That mindset carries into everything I do.

I’m also a father first.
A businessman by necessity.
An anime watcher by choice.
And an overall laid-back individual—until you mess with my kids.

I’m not loud for attention. I’m not reckless. I’m strategic, observant, and intentional. I believe in responsibility, self-mastery, and standing on truth even when it’s uncomfortable. Most of the time, I mind my business, work hard, and move with respect.

But when it comes to my children, I don’t fold.
I don’t disappear.
And I don’t let systems, narratives, or other people rewrite my role as a father.

This space exists because I’ve lived what many people are going through—family court, separation, silence, confusion, and the moment where you realize something isn’t right. I didn’t come here as a victim. I came here as a student of the system who decided to learn, document, and share.

I’m telling my story in parts—not for sympathy, but for clarity, connection, and truth. If you’re here to learn, listen, heal, or support others, you’re in the right place. If you’re here just to watch—stay anyway.

More of my story is coming.
And I stand on everything I say.
