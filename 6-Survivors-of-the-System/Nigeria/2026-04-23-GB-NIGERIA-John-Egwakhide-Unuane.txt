 "Sometimes, all that is needed is a real story to spark a real change."
My name is John Egwakhide Unuane, and I have always been drawn to understanding people, not just by what they say, but by what they feel and often struggle to express.

I think a lot about the things people don’t say. The quiet struggles. The unspoken questions. The moments that don’t make it to the surface but define so much of who we are. Over time, it became clear to me that many of these stories are left untold, not because they are unimportant, but because they are uncomfortable, complex, or simply overlooked.

Yet, those are often the stories that matter the most.

The Intersection of Data and Heart
I’ve spent much of my life navigating two seemingly different worlds. In one, I am a director and a data science student, looking at the world through the lens of systems, fundraising targets, and analytical models. In the other, I am a musician and a storyteller, searching for the perfect harmony in a choir arrangement or the right emotion for an animation.

For a long time, I kept these worlds separate. But I’ve realized that the most important truths; the real "reality checks" are found when those two worlds collide. My own story is one of balancing logic with melody, and social advocacy with personal growth. On this blog, I’ll be sharing those chapters of my life as they happen, unfiltered and in real-time.

A Mirror, Not a Mask
Reality Check with John is a space where I give those stories a voice. This platform is built on honesty; telling stories as they are, without dressing them up or watering them down. It is built on the belief that every life carries a story worth telling. Some are loud, some are quiet, some are still unfolding, but all are real.

In a world of "social media dress-ups" that hide pain and cloak reality, this blog is intended to be a mirror. It is a place to confront reality, question norms, and connect through shared human experiences.

What We Will Explore Together
Through my own personal journey and the shared voices of others, we will explore:

The Unpolished Truth: Struggles, growth, failures, and the resilience I find in my own work and studies.

The Everyday Victories: Those small, significant moments in our communities that often go unnoticed.

Society Unplugged: Reflections on our world and our "State of the Nation" through a more honest, unfiltered lens.

Here, stories are not polished to perfection. They are authentic and relatable. Each piece is an invitation to pause, reflect, and see the world for what it truly is.

Join Me
I’m starting this journey because I believe in the power of the human experience when it is shared without a filter. I want to hear your reality checks, too. What are the stories you’ve been hesitant to tell?

Thank you for being here at the very beginning. Let’s look into the mirror together.

— John Egwakhide Unuane
